Understanding the Business

New Wheels is a vehicle subscription company that lets customers subscribe to cars rather than buy them outright. The goal of this project was to analyze a year of order data across 10 business questions and give the leadership team a clear picture of company health.

  • 1,000 total orders across 4 quarters
  • 994 unique customers across 49 US states
  • Data spans order details, customer info, vehicle specs, and feedback
  • Multiple SQL tables joined using JOINs and CTEs
2

Where Are Customers Located?

The first question was to understand the geographic distribution of customers. This helps identify which states drive the most business and where there may be untapped markets.

  • Texas and California tied for most customers — 97 each
  • Florida had 86 customers, New York had 69
  • 49 states were represented in total
  • Lower-population states had far fewer customers — a marketing opportunity
SQL Query
SELECT c.state, COUNT(DISTINCT o.customer_id) AS customers_in_state
FROM order_t o
JOIN customer_t c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
GROUP BY c.state
ORDER BY customers_in_state DESC;

Top 5 Vehicle Makers by Orders

Understanding which vehicle brands customers prefer helps New Wheels make smarter inventory decisions. A small number of brands dominated order volume.

  • Chevrolet led with 83 orders — the most of any brand
  • Ford was second with 63 orders
  • Toyota (52), Pontiac (50), and Dodge (50) rounded out the top 5
  • These 5 brands account for a significant share of total orders
SQL Query
SELECT p.vehicle_maker,
       COUNT(o.product_id) AS total_orders
FROM order_t o
JOIN product_t p ON o.product_id = p.product_id
GROUP BY p.vehicle_maker
ORDER BY total_orders DESC
LIMIT 5;

Customer Ratings Are Declining

One of the most alarming findings was a consistent decline in customer satisfaction across all four quarters. Ratings dropped from 3.55 in Q1 to just 2.40 by Q4 — a steep fall that signals a serious problem.

  • Q1 average rating: 3.55 — the highest of the year
  • Q2: 3.35 — already declining after the first quarter
  • Q3: 2.96 — dropped below 3.0 for the first time
  • Q4: 2.40 — a 32% drop from Q1, a critical warning sign
SQL Query (using CTE)
WITH feedback_numeric AS (
  SELECT order_id, quarter_number,
    CASE customer_feedback
      WHEN 'Very Bad'  THEN 1
      WHEN 'Bad'       THEN 2
      WHEN 'Okay'      THEN 3
      WHEN 'Good'      THEN 4
      WHEN 'Very Good' THEN 5
    END AS feedback_score
  FROM order_t
)
SELECT quarter_number,
       ROUND(AVG(feedback_score), 2) AS avg_rating_per_quarter
FROM feedback_numeric
GROUP BY quarter_number
ORDER BY quarter_number;

Feedback Is Shifting Toward "Very Bad"

Beyond the average rating, the breakdown of feedback categories tells an even more alarming story. "Very Bad" feedback grew from 11% in Q1 to 31% in Q4, while "Very Good" feedback collapsed from 30% to just 10%.

  • Q1: Very Bad = 10.97%, Very Good = 30% — customers were satisfied
  • Q2: Very Bad started rising to 14.89%, Very Good fell to 28.63%
  • Q3: Very Bad at 17.9%, Very Good dropped sharply to 16.59%
  • Q4: Very Bad hit 30.65%, Very Good collapsed to 10.05%

Orders and Revenue Are Both Falling

The business was not just losing customer satisfaction — it was losing orders and revenue quarter after quarter. Q1 to Q4 saw a 36% drop in orders and a massive revenue decline.

  • Q1: 310 orders, $18.0M revenue — the strongest quarter
  • Q2: 262 orders, $13.1M — a 27% revenue drop from Q1
  • Q3: 229 orders, $8.9M — another 32% drop
  • Q4: 199 orders, $8.6M — the weakest quarter of the year
SQL Query
SELECT o.quarter_number,
  ROUND(SUM(o.quantity * p.vehicle_price * (1 - o.discount)), 2)
    AS net_revenue,
  COUNT(*) AS total_orders
FROM order_t o
JOIN product_t p ON o.product_id = p.product_id
GROUP BY o.quarter_number
ORDER BY o.quarter_number;

Shipping Times Are Getting Worse

A key driver of the declining satisfaction scores was shipping delays. Average shipping time tripled from Q1 to Q4, jumping from 57 days to 174 days — an unacceptable level for any subscription business.

  • Q1: avg shipping time of 57.17 days
  • Q2: jumped to 71.11 days
  • Q3: surged to 117.76 days
  • Q4: peaked at 174.10 days — 3x longer than Q1
SQL Query
SELECT quarter_number,
  ROUND(AVG(DATEDIFF(ship_date, order_date)), 2)
    AS avg_shipping_days
FROM order_t
WHERE ship_date IS NOT NULL
  AND order_date IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY quarter_number
ORDER BY quarter_number;

Business Recommendations

The data told a clear story: New Wheels was in decline across every key metric. The following recommendations were made to help the company turn things around.

🚚 Fix Shipping Operations

Shipping times tripled in one year. This is almost certainly driving the satisfaction collapse. Investigate supply chain bottlenecks and set a maximum acceptable shipping window.

📦 Stock Popular Brands

Chevrolet, Ford, and Toyota drove the most orders. Ensure consistent inventory of these brands — stockouts may be contributing to order drops and delays.

📍 Expand in High-Demand States

Texas, California, and Florida account for a large share of customers. Increase marketing and inventory availability in these states to maximize revenue from high-demand regions.

💳 Leverage Credit Card Partnerships

Laser and Mastercard offer the highest discounts. Partner with these issuers for co-marketing campaigns and sign-up incentives to attract new subscribers.

📣 Promote in Low-Activity States

Many states had very few customers despite being large markets. Targeted digital advertising in these states could unlock significant untapped revenue.

⭐ Monitor Ratings in Real Time

The rating decline was gradual but went unaddressed all year. Implement a real-time feedback dashboard so leadership can catch satisfaction drops before they become critical.
