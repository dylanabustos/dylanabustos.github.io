Understanding the Problem

The US visa application process is complex and time-consuming. The goal was to build a model that could predict whether an application would be certified or denied, helping applicants and employers understand the key decision factors.

  • 25,480 visa applications in the dataset
  • 66.8% were certified, 33.2% were denied
  • Features include education, job experience, region, continent, and wage
  • Target variable: case_status (Certified / Denied)

Where Are Applicants Coming From?

The vast majority of visa applications came from Asia, driven largely by tech and engineering workers from countries like India and China applying for US work visas.

  • Asia accounted for 66.2% of all applications (16,861)
  • Europe was second with 3,732 applications
  • Oceania had the fewest with just 192 applications

Does Education Level Matter?

Education level was one of the strongest predictors of visa approval. Higher education was strongly associated with certification, with doctorate holders having by far the best outcomes.

  • Doctorate holders had the highest certification rate at 87.2%
  • Master's degree holders had a 78.6% certification rate
  • High school education had only a 34% certification rate

Does Job Experience Help?

Having prior job experience made a meaningful difference in visa outcomes. Applicants with experience were significantly more likely to be certified.

  • Applicants with job experience had a 74.5% certification rate
  • Applicants without had a 56.1% certification rate
  • An 18.4 percentage point difference — a significant factor

Certification Rate by Region

The region where the job is located also played a role. Midwest positions had the highest certification rate while Island regions had the lowest.

  • Midwest had the highest certification rate at 75.5%
  • South had a 70% certification rate
  • Island regions had the lowest at 60.3%

6

Building the Models

Two ensemble methods were used: Bagging (trains multiple models on random data subsets) and Boosting (builds models sequentially, each correcting the last). Both were compared to find the best performer.

  • Bagging — reduces variance by averaging multiple model predictions
  • Boosting — reduces bias by learning from previous model mistakes
  • Both models achieved 75–78% accuracy on the test set
  • Key features: education level, job experience, continent, prevailing wage
7

Conclusion & Recommendations

The model successfully identified key drivers of visa approval. These insights can help both employers and applicants understand what makes a strong application.

  • Education is the strongest predictor — advanced degrees dramatically improve approval odds
  • Job experience significantly improves approval chances — highlight it in applications
  • Midwest positions have the best approval rates — consider regional job placement
  • European applicants have a 79.2% approval rate — highest of any continent
