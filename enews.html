Understanding the Problem

E-news Express was losing subscribers due to a poorly performing landing page. They redesigned the page and wanted to know: does the new design actually convert more visitors into subscribers? An A/B test was run to answer this scientifically.

  • 100 users randomly split into two equal groups of 50
  • Control group (50 users) saw the old landing page
  • Treatment group (50 users) saw the new redesigned landing page
  • Key metrics: conversion rate and time spent on page
2

The A/B Test Setup

A proper A/B test requires a clear hypothesis before any data is collected. Two hypotheses were defined: one about conversion rate and one about time spent on the page.

  • H₀ (null): The new page has no effect on conversion rate
  • H₁ (alternative): The new page increases conversion rate
  • Significance level (alpha) set at 0.05 — standard for business experiments
  • A second test was run on time spent on page as a supporting metric
3

Conversion Rate Results

The results were clear — the new landing page performed significantly better. The treatment group converted at a 66% rate compared to just 42% for the control group.

  • New page (treatment): 66% conversion rate — 33 out of 50 users subscribed
  • Old page (control): 42% conversion rate — 21 out of 50 users subscribed
  • A 24 percentage point improvement — a 57% relative lift in conversions
  • Statistical test confirmed this difference is not due to chance

4

Time Spent on Page

Beyond just conversions, users who saw the new page also spent significantly more time reading it. This suggests the new design was more engaging, not just better at prompting signups.

  • Treatment group spent an avg of 6.22 minutes on the new page
  • Control group spent an avg of 4.53 minutes on the old page
  • A 37% increase in time spent — the new page was far more engaging
  • Converted users averaged 6.62 min vs 3.92 min for non-converted users

5

Conversion Rate by Language

The dataset also included the preferred language of each user. English speakers converted at the highest rate, suggesting the new page resonates most strongly with English-speaking audiences.

  • English speakers had the highest conversion rate at 65.6%
  • Spanish speakers converted at 52.9%
  • French speakers had the lowest conversion rate at 44.1%
  • A localized version of the new page could boost French and Spanish conversions

6

Hypothesis Testing Results

Two statistical tests were run — a two-proportion z-test for conversion rate and a two-sample t-test for time spent. Both tests rejected the null hypothesis, confirming the new page is a genuine improvement.

  • Conversion rate test: p-value below 0.05 — reject the null hypothesis
  • Time spent test: p-value below 0.05 — reject the null hypothesis
  • Both results are statistically significant at the 95% confidence level
  • Conclusion: the new landing page is genuinely better, not a random result
7

Conclusion & Recommendations

The A/B test provided strong statistical evidence that the new landing page outperforms the old one on every metric measured.

  • Roll out the new landing page to all users immediately — the data supports it
  • The 57% lift in conversions represents a major revenue opportunity at scale
  • Invest in French and Spanish localization to capture lower-performing language groups
  • Continue A/B testing — further iterations could push conversion rates even higher
