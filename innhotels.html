Understanding the Problem

INN Hotels Group was experiencing a high rate of booking cancellations, which directly impacts revenue. The goal was to build a model that could predict whether a booking would be canceled, allowing the hotel to take proactive steps to retain customers or adjust pricing.

  • 36,275 total bookings in the dataset
  • 32.8% of all bookings were canceled — a significant revenue risk
  • 19 features including lead time, room type, meal plan, and market segment
  • Target variable: booking_status (Canceled / Not_Canceled)

2

Cleaning & Preparing the Data

Before building models, the data was cleaned and prepared. Categorical variables like room type, meal plan, and market segment were encoded into numbers so the machine learning models could process them.

  • No missing values found in the dataset
  • Categorical variables were encoded using label encoding
  • Data was split 70/30 into training and testing sets
  • Features were scaled to ensure fair model comparisons
3

What Factors Drive Cancellations?

Before building the model, exploratory analysis was done to understand which factors were most associated with cancellations. Two key drivers stood out: lead time and special requests.

  • Canceled bookings had an avg lead time of 139 days vs 59 days for kept bookings
  • Customers who canceled made far fewer special requests (0.33 vs 0.76 avg)
  • Online bookings had the highest cancellation rate at 36.5%
  • Corporate bookings had the lowest cancellation rate at just 10.9%

Cancellations by Month

Looking at cancellation rates across the year revealed a strong seasonal pattern. Summer months had significantly higher cancellation rates, which has major implications for revenue planning.

  • July had the highest cancellation rate at 45%
  • January had the lowest at just 2.4%
  • Cancellation rates peaked in summer (June–August) and dropped in winter
  • This suggests the hotel should apply stricter policies during peak season

Cancellation Rate by Room Type

Different room types had notably different cancellation rates. Understanding which room types are most at risk helps the hotel prioritize overbooking strategies and targeted retention efforts.

  • Room Type 6 had the highest cancellation rate at 42%
  • Room Type 7 had the lowest at 22.8%
  • Higher priced rooms (Type 6 avg $182) tended to have higher cancellation rates

Building the Models

Two classification models were built and compared: Logistic Regression (a simpler, more interpretable model) and a Decision Tree (which can capture more complex patterns in the data).

  • Logistic Regression — good baseline, interpretable coefficients
  • Decision Tree — better at capturing non-linear relationships in the data
  • Both models evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score
  • The Decision Tree outperformed Logistic Regression on this dataset
7

Conclusion & Recommendations

The model successfully identified bookings at high risk of cancellation. Combined with the exploratory findings, several actionable recommendations were made for INN Hotels Group.

  • Flag bookings with lead times over 100 days as high cancellation risk
  • Introduce stricter cancellation policies during summer months (June–August)
  • Encourage special requests at booking time — strongly associated with retention
  • Offer incentives to online bookers to reduce the 36.5% cancellation rate
  • Consider overbooking strategies for Room Type 6 given its 42% cancellation rate
