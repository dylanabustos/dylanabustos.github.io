Understanding the Data

The dataset contains 1,898 food delivery orders from FoodHub. Each row represents one order and includes details about the customer, restaurant, cuisine, cost, delivery time, and rating.

  • 1,898 total orders across 14 cuisine types
  • Key columns: cuisine type, cost, delivery time, rating, day of week
  • Orders split between weekdays and weekends
  • Ratings of 3, 4, or 5 — many customers did not leave a rating
2

Cleaning the Data

Before diving into analysis, the data was checked for issues that could affect the results. The main issue found was that many ratings were listed as "Not given" rather than a number.

  • 736 out of 1,898 orders had no rating provided (38.8%)
  • No duplicate orders found in the dataset
  • "Not given" ratings were excluded from average rating calculations
  • All other columns were complete with no missing values
3

Which Cuisines Are Most Popular?

The first question was — what are customers actually ordering? Understanding cuisine popularity helps FoodHub decide which restaurant partnerships to prioritize and where to focus marketing efforts.

  • American food was ordered most — 584 orders (30.8% of all orders)
  • Japanese was second most popular with 470 orders
  • Together American and Japanese make up over 55% of all orders
  • Vietnamese and Korean cuisines had the fewest orders

4

When Are Orders Being Placed?

Understanding when customers order is critical for staffing, inventory, and targeted promotions. The data was split between weekday and weekend orders to see if there was a meaningful difference.

  • 71.2% of all orders were placed on weekends
  • Only 28.8% of orders came in on weekdays
  • Weekend delivery times were faster — avg 22.5 min vs 28.3 min on weekdays
  • This suggests weekday orders may face more delays or traffic

Top Restaurants by Order Volume

A small number of restaurants were responsible for a large portion of all orders. Identifying these top performers helps FoodHub understand which partnerships are most valuable.

  • Shake Shack led with 219 orders — the most of any restaurant
  • The top 10 restaurants accounted for 887 orders (46.7% of all orders)
  • Blue Ribbon brand appeared 3 times in the top 10

How Are Customers Rating Their Orders?

Customer ratings tell us how satisfied people are with their orders. Looking at average ratings by cuisine reveals which food types consistently delight customers.

  • Spanish cuisine had the highest avg rating at 4.83 out of 5
  • Indian cuisine was close behind at 4.54
  • Vietnamese cuisine had the lowest avg rating at 4.0
  • All cuisines scored above 4.0 — overall customer satisfaction was high

What Do Most Orders Cost?

Understanding the distribution of order costs helps identify the most common price range customers spend in, which can guide promotional pricing and discount strategies.

  • Most orders fell in the $10–$15 range (614 orders)
  • Orders under $20 made up nearly 71% of all orders
  • Very few orders exceeded $30
  • Average order cost was $16.50
Order Cost Distribution — hover for details
8

Conclusion & Recommendations

Based on the full analysis of 1,898 orders, several clear patterns emerged that FoodHub can act on to improve customer experience and grow revenue.

  • Focus weekend marketing on American and Japanese cuisines — they drive over 55% of all orders
  • Investigate why weekday delivery times are 26% slower than weekends and address the bottleneck
  • Strengthen partnerships with Shake Shack and The Meatball Shop — they generate the most volume
  • Promote Spanish and Indian cuisines more — they have the highest customer satisfaction scores
  • Consider targeted promotions for orders in the $15–$25 range to increase average order value
