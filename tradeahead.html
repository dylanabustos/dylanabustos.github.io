Understanding the Problem

Trade & Ahead wanted to group stocks into clusters based on their financial characteristics rather than just their sector labels. This helps investors identify stocks with similar behavior patterns for diversification and portfolio construction.

  • 340 S&P 500 stocks across 11 sectors
  • Features: price, volatility, ROE, cash ratio, P/E ratio, P/B ratio, and more
  • Goal: find natural groupings of stocks that share financial characteristics
  • No predefined labels — this is unsupervised learning

Exploring Stock Prices by Sector

Before clustering, the data was explored to understand the distribution of stock prices and volatility across sectors. Health Care and Consumer Discretionary had the highest average prices.

  • Health Care had the highest avg stock price at $132
  • Consumer Discretionary was close behind at $128 (driven by Amazon, Priceline)
  • Telecommunications Services had the lowest avg price at $33
  • Energy had the highest avg volatility at 2.57

Which Sectors Are Most Volatile?

Volatility measures how much a stock's price fluctuates. Higher volatility means higher risk but also potentially higher reward. Energy stocks were by far the most volatile in the dataset.

  • Energy sector had the highest avg volatility at 2.57
  • Materials were second most volatile at 1.82
  • Utilities had the lowest volatility at 1.12 — a traditionally defensive sector
  • Consumer Staples also had low volatility at 1.15

Applying K-Means Clustering

K-Means clustering groups stocks into a specified number of clusters by minimizing the distance between each stock and its cluster center. The optimal number of clusters was found using the elbow method.

  • K-Means assigns each stock to the nearest cluster center
  • The elbow method was used to find the optimal number of clusters
  • Stocks were scaled before clustering to prevent large-value features from dominating
  • Clusters were analyzed to understand what each group represents
5

Applying Hierarchical Clustering

Hierarchical clustering builds a tree of clusters, allowing you to see how stocks relate to each other at different levels of similarity. The quality was measured using the cophenetic correlation score.

  • Built a dendrogram showing how stocks merge into clusters
  • Tested multiple distance metrics and linkage methods
  • Euclidean distance + average linkage gave the best cophenetic score of 0.94
  • A score of 0.94 indicates an excellent fit between the dendrogram and original data
6

Conclusion & Recommendations

The clustering analysis revealed natural groupings of stocks that cut across traditional sector boundaries. These clusters can be used to build more diversified portfolios.

  • Avoid over-concentration in high-volatility Energy and Materials clusters
  • Utilities and Consumer Staples form a natural low-risk defensive cluster
  • High-growth tech and healthcare stocks clustered together despite different sectors
  • Use cluster membership rather than sector alone when building a diversified portfolio
